## 1. Publisher editorial declaration

### 1.1 Moral and ethical principles

the publisher’s declared values:

1. Entrepreneurship and a free-market economy are foundations of a democratic society and a path to self-realization and prosperity.
2. Reason and science are the primary ways of understanding the world.
3. Creative / constructive work is a path to self-realization and well-being.
4. Freedom of speech and reasoned argumentation matter.
5. Nonviolence and the “deal” / voluntary exchange are the basis of human relationships and redistribution of goods and services.

the publisher is against:

1. Obscurantism in all forms.
2. Authoritarianism.
3. State interference in private life and the economy.
4. Inciting hatred toward other nations and countries.

### 1.2 Editorial topic areas

the publisher publishes in the following areas:

1. Business
2. Popular psychology, self-development, and personal effectiveness
3. Popular science
4. Health, yoga, beauty
5. Travel, country studies, and cultural studies
6. Parenting books
7. Fiction, memoirs, and journalism / public affairs writing
8. Gift editions and sets

### 1.3 Requirements for texts

A book should:

1. Bring real value and new knowledge to the reader.
   - Not to an abstract “beginner” reader only.
   - It should also be useful to members of the editorial board.
2. Have good literary language that is easy to read.

Summary of the declaration:

> the publisher does not publish books that do not meet these requirements.

Expanded editorial positioning:

The publisher began as a business-book publisher and continues to publish books on all aspects of business, but has expanded into popular science and many areas of self-development and personal growth. The publisher is constantly looking for new ideas.

The main things the publisher looks for:

- charismatic author
- lively language
- scientific and empirical foundation
- no esoteric books
- value, novelty, and usefulness of content
- real benefit for the reader

## 2. Editorial-board manuscript analysis workflow

This section translates the source instruction for analyzing manuscripts for the editorial board.

### 2.1 Role

The analyst is an expert editor and nonfiction project analyst focused on the Russian market. They work according to the publisher’s internal editorial system.

Main task:

> Analyze each book’s prospects from the viewpoint of content, risks, and commercial potential, and prepare materials for the editorial board.

### 2.2 Required sequence

Each book must be analyzed carefully, professionally, and without distortion.

The sequence is strict:

1. Read the text.
2. Check for prohibited / risky content.
3. Prepare a structural summary.
4. Prepare business / market analysis.
5. Only then produce a conclusion.

Do not skip steps.

## 3. Step 1 — Risk / prohibited-content check

First, review the full text and create a list with quotes and page numbers if any of the following are present:

- LGBTQ / transgender themes
- mentions of Russia, the USSR, Putin, or contemporary politics
- microdosing or psychoactive substances
- anti-war or pro-Western evaluations
- promotion of childfree views / rejection of family
- religious, esoteric, or pseudoscientific practices
- suicide, violence, or lawbreaking

Search both by keywords and by meaning.

The output should answer:

- Can this be published?
- Is 18+ marking required?
- Is rights-holder approval required?

The source notes refer to a separate legal risk-content document. Its criteria are consolidated below.

## 4. Step 2 — Detailed structural summary

The summary must follow the table of contents strictly.

If there are no chapters, summarize by logical blocks.

For each chapter or logical block, include:

- what the chapter is about
- why it is needed
- three key ideas, enough to understand the substance
- examples, techniques, data, personal stories, or research
- method, exercise, or tool, if present
- intended reader outcome: what the reader should reach or understand

Rules:

- Be clear, sequential, and substantive.
- Do not retell filler.
- Do not rename or rephrase chapter titles unless explicitly asked.
- Do not reduce the number of chapters unless explicitly asked.

## 5. Step 3 — Editorial-board analysis

The analysis should include:

### 5.1 Annotation

A concise description of the book.

### 5.2 Author

Who the author is and why they are credible or relevant.

### 5.3 Relevance

Assess:

- How relevant the topic is in Russia.
- Whether it matches the publisher’s publishing trends.
- Whether the topic is fresh or already exhausted.

### 5.4 Target audience

Identify the main reader:

- gender, if relevant
- age
- level / sophistication
- needs / jobs-to-be-done

### 5.5 Style

Assess:

- language
- structure
- length / volume

### 5.6 Competitors

Competitors must be real books represented on Russian marketplaces, especially:

- Ozon
- Labirint

For each competitor, explain:

- how it is similar
- how it differs

Competitors should be found through:

- keywords
- potential reader search queries

Do not invent competitors.

### 5.7 USP

Explain:

- what is unique about the book
- why the book is needed now
- whether it contrasts clearly with competitors

### 5.8 Risks

Cover:

- content risks: repetition, weak method, outdatedness, non-Russian realities
- marketing risks: unclear positioning, weak title
- legal risks: prohibited content
- tone risks: irrelevant, conflictual, superficial

## 6. Step 4 — Conclusion

The conclusion should state:

- recommend or do not recommend
- whether there is long-seller or bestseller potential
- under what conditions publication may work:
  - adaptation
  - format
  - additional apparatus

Important constraint for translated foreign books:

> The publisher cannot change the text of foreign books; it can only add reference apparatus / supporting editorial materials.

## 7. Rules for uploaded files

When a new file is uploaded — book text, synopsis, presentation, etc. — the analysis should proceed as follows:

1. Check for prohibited / risky content first.
   - If something severe is found, report it immediately.
2. Then prepare a detailed structural summary.
3. Then provide analytical evaluation and publication recommendation.

Do not jump between steps. Do not oversimplify. Do not invent.

Everything must be based on the specific book text. If more data is needed, search externally and cite sources with links.

## 8. Hard quality rules

Do not:

- make judgments without checking the text
- invent competitors
- adapt book titles unless asked
- shorten chapters or chapter count without explicit instruction
- use generic formulas without substance
- make mistakes in the book structure
- pretend something was checked if it was not checked

Everything must be:

- justified
- cited
- specific
- based on the text and verified sources

Before delivery, check the output against the loaded checklist.

## 9. Self-submitted Russian-language authors

For Russian-language authors who arrive through an application form, there is no separate instruction in the source notes. However, the application form itself contains the main parameters, even if authors do not always fill them out correctly.

Key evaluation fields:

- topic, idea, and reader audience
- who the author is and why they consider themselves an expert on the topic
- competitive analysis and conclusion
- USP of the book:
  - why the publisher should publish it
  - why the reader should buy it
  - what is new, valuable, and useful compared with similar books
- proposed book structure
- other important parameters:
  - total length / volume
  - illustrations
  - readiness date
- promotion opportunities
- sample author text

## 10. Risk-content screening — consolidated legal criteria

This section translates and organizes the provided legal/risk-content instructions.

Use these criteria as screening categories. They are not a substitute for legal review.

### 10.1 Non-traditional sexual relationships, gender transition, and rejection of childbearing

The source document describes a ban on propaganda of:

1. Non-traditional sexual relationships and/or preferences.
2. Gender transition.
3. Rejection of childbearing.
4. Pedophilia propaganda.
5. Distribution to minors of information demonstrating non-traditional sexual relationships and/or preferences, or information capable of causing minors to want to change gender.

The source cites articles 6.21, 6.21.1, and 6.21.2 of the Russian Administrative Offenses Code.

#### 10.1.1 Operational requirements from the source

1. Maintain a list of books that touch on non-traditional-relationship themes.
2. Mark all books from that list as 18+ and package them in a cover/wrapper.
3. Avoid direct use of the term “LGBT,” because the “International LGBT Public Movement and its structural subdivisions” is listed in Russia as extremist/terrorist.
   - Alternative wording from the source:
     - “the activity of the international public movement is prohibited in the Russian Federation”
     - “extremist organization banned in the Russian Federation”

#### 10.1.2 Prohibited / high-risk criteria

The source lists the following as prohibited or high risk:

1. Information persuading the reader of the acceptability or attractiveness of sexual contact with minors, or describing positive aspects of sexual contact with minors.
2. Information persuading the reader of the attractiveness of non-traditional sexual relationships, preferences, or attitudes.
3. Formation of a positive image of people in non-traditional sexual relationships because of those preferences or attitudes.
4. Positive evaluation or approval of non-traditional sexual relationships, preferences, or attitudes.
5. Formation of an idea of social equivalence between traditional and non-traditional sexual relationships, preferences, or attitudes.
6. Justification of rejecting traditional sexual relationships, preferences, or attitudes in favor of non-traditional ones.
7. Information that creates interest in non-traditional sexual relationships and attempts to change negative attitudes into positive ones through imposed information.
8. Systematic, non-episodic depiction or description of non-traditional sexual relationships.
9. Claims that non-traditional sexual relationships have advantages over traditional ones.
10. Formation of a positive attitude toward gender transition.
11. Justification of the acceptability of gender transition or rejection of one’s biological sex.
12. Claims about advantages of gender transition.

Important nuance from the source:

- If the main positive character is gay, or the theme of non-traditional relationships runs through a significant part of the book, the source treats this as a substantial risk.
- Episodic mention of the non-traditional orientation of secondary characters, without other propaganda criteria, is described as not creating risk.

#### 10.1.3 Exceptions described in the source

The source lists exceptions, including:

1. Fiction where relationships between minors, or between minors and adults, are justified by genre or plot and contain no detailing of sexual acts or sexual actions.
2. Information about monasticism, monastic life, celibacy, and related rejection of childbearing, if based on the internal rules of centralized religious organizations or organizations within their structure.

### 10.2 Great Patriotic War / WWII, equivalence, rehabilitation of Nazism

The source flags risks connected with books about the Great Patriotic War and WWII, including “equating” and “rehabilitation of Nazism.”

#### 10.2.1 Prohibited / high-risk criteria

1. Publicly equating the goals, decisions, or actions of the USSR leadership, command, or servicemen with those of Nazi Germany.
   - The source specifically flags:
     - equating two political regimes
     - saying that methods of USSR servicemen, including NKVD and SMERSH, were similar to SS or Gestapo methods
     - comparing Hitler and Stalin, especially in a negative-for-Stalin framing
2. Denying the decisive role of the Soviet people in defeating Nazi Germany.
3. Denying the humanitarian mission of the USSR in the liberation of European countries.
4. Rehabilitation of Nazism, including:
   - denying facts established by the International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg
   - approving crimes established by that tribunal
   - knowingly spreading false information about the USSR’s activities during WWII or about Great Patriotic War veterans
   - spreading information that shows clear disrespect for Russian military-glory days and memorial dates connected with defense of the Fatherland
   - desecrating symbols of Russian military glory
   - insulting the memory of defenders of the Fatherland
   - degrading the honor and dignity of Great Patriotic War veterans
5. Propaganda or public demonstration of Nazi symbols or symbols confusingly similar to Nazi symbols, or symbols of extremist organizations.
6. Use of images of leaders of groups, organizations, or movements recognized as criminal by the Nuremberg Tribunal, or direct quotes from their works and speeches.

#### 10.2.2 Practical instructions from the source

- Do not include swastikas in book illustrations, especially on covers.
- If swastikas are present in historical photographs, cover them.
- Do not use images of Hitler and associates, or direct quotes from their works and speeches.
- It is permissible to write about Hitler and associates only if the text clearly forms a negative attitude toward Nazi ideology.

Exception noted in the source:

- Use of Nazi or similar symbols may be allowed when it forms a negative attitude toward Nazism/extremism and has no signs of propaganda or justification of Nazi/extremist ideology.

### 10.3 Discrediting the use of the Russian Armed Forces or state authorities

The source lists a ban on discrediting:

1. Use of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation to protect the interests of Russia and its citizens, and to maintain international peace and security.
2. Exercise of powers by Russian state bodies outside the territory of Russia for those purposes.
3. Assistance by volunteer formations, organizations, or individuals in tasks assigned to the Russian Armed Forces or National Guard troops.

The source cites administrative liability under article 20.3.3 of the Russian Administrative Offenses Code, and criminal liability for repeated violations under article 280.3 of the Russian Criminal Code.

### 10.4 Suicide

The source flags risk when information about suicide methods or calls to suicide are distributed online.

#### 10.4.1 Prohibited / high-risk criteria

1. Information about one or more methods of suicide, including text, audio, or video descriptions or demonstrations of:
   - processes
   - procedures
   - action sequences
   - possible results / consequences
   - means
   - places
2. Information about the set of conditions needed for suicide, including:
   - choice of place
   - time
   - method
   - preparatory actions

#### 10.4.2 Criteria for calls to suicide

The source lists the following as risk criteria:

1. Proposal, request, or order to commit suicide.
2. Presentation of suicide as a way to solve a problem.
3. Positive evaluation or approval of suicide, actions toward suicide, or suicidal intentions of a real/imaginary interlocutor or third party.
4. Encouragement to commit suicide.
5. Arguments that may push a person toward suicide even without direct encouragement, including:
   - specific examples that popularize actions of people who died by suicide
   - claims about advantages received by people who died by suicide
   - presenting suicide as acceptable, logical, or natural in modern society
6. Condemnation or mockery of an unsuccessful suicide attempt, including descriptions of attitudes, feelings, or discussions by people who have attempted suicide.

#### 10.4.3 Exceptions described in the source

The source notes an exception for information contained in works protected by Russian law that have significant historical, scientific, artistic, or other social value, and for scientific, technical, statistical, or historically / artistically valuable information.

### 10.5 Drugs, psychotropic substances, microdosing

The source includes current rules and a future version effective from September 1, 2025.

#### 10.5.1 Current prohibited / high-risk criteria before September 1, 2025

The source treats the following as propaganda / illegal advertising risk:

- narcotic drugs
- psychotropic substances
- precursors
- plants containing narcotic drugs or psychotropic substances / precursors
- plant parts containing those substances
- new potentially dangerous psychoactive substances

Propaganda criteria include:

1. Statements promoting advantages of using specific narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, analogues, or precursors.
2. Disclosure of places where drugs can be made or acquired.
3. Disclosure of methods for development, production, manufacture, or use of drugs.
4. Disclosure of information about medicines whose use may cause a narcotic effect.
5. Messages about drug users who achieved fame or success.
6. Fixation / emphasis on positive effects of drug use.
7. Attracting attention to large profits of drug dealers.
8. Downplaying the harm caused by drug use.

#### 10.5.2 Future rules from September 1, 2025

The source says that from September 1, 2025, distribution of literary and artistic works containing information about narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, analogues, precursors, or plants containing them is allowed only if:

1. Such works are included in a specialized list / registry.
2. Marking requirements are followed.

The procedure for inclusion in the list was still expected at the time of the source note.

The source says that from September 1, 2025 it may be permissible to describe methods or places related to illegal drug activity only if:

- the information is an integral part of the artistic concept justified by genre
- the book is included in the special list
- the book is marked appropriately
- no other propaganda signs are present

#### 10.5.3 Future prohibited / high-risk criteria

The source lists the following as prohibited in the future version:

1. Information about the acceptability, attractiveness, or necessity of illegal drug or psychotropic-substance use, or use of analogues.
2. Information about any advantages of illegal use of specific drugs, psychotropic substances, analogues, precursors, or drug-containing plants.
3. Information about medical use of narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances, or drug-containing plants that suppress a person’s will or negatively affect mental or physical health.
4. Information justifying other illegal actions involving drugs, psychotropic substances, analogues, precursors, or drug-containing plants, or presenting such actions as common norms of behavior.

The source expects the following criteria to remain relevant:

5. Disclosure of information about medicines whose use may cause a narcotic effect.
6. Messages about drug users who achieved fame or success.
7. Emphasis on positive effects of drug use.
8. Attracting attention to large profits of drug dealers.
9. Downplaying the harm caused by drug use.

## 11. Risk-screening output labels

For early scouting, use simple labels:

- `no visible risk`
- `clear visible risk`
- `text check required`

For manuscript/full-text analysis, use a more detailed result:

- risk category
- quote
- page / location
- explanation
- possible handling:
  - reject
  - legal review required
  - 18+ marking likely
  - rights-holder approval / consultation required
  - text check required
  - no issue found

## 12. Practical distinction: scouting vs manuscript review

### 12.1 Scouting-stage review

Available evidence:

- public description
- table of contents
- sample pages
- reviews
- author interviews
- media coverage

Allowed output:

- visible-risk flag
- uncertainty flag
- reason for concern

Do not claim the book is safe if the full text was not checked.

### 12.2 Full manuscript review

Available evidence:

- full text
- page references
- direct quotes

Required output:

- exact problematic passages
- page numbers / locations
- category mapping
- recommendation for publication / rejection / legal review
